Versions 0.18.0 and 0.19.0 add selected source coordinates for 1EHZ, 1HR2 and 7BG9, offline importers and reusable molecular views. The source files, selections, hashes and limitations are documented beside the fixtures. PDB archive data is distributed under CC0; source-backed camera transformations are not computed folding trajectories. These coordinate fixtures were introduced after the schematic-only versions described below.

Version 0.15.0 adds a reusable molecular visual-check template using the existing schematic actors and shared lesson shell. Its authored poses, motion and enlarged captured views are software and presentation checks, not biological measurements, molecular trajectories or kinetic predictions. The optional QA helper is activated with `?qa=1` and does not supply scientific validation.

Version 0.14.0 extends the original molecular drawings with histone-tail chemistry, Mediator/PIC, further CRISPR families and RNA-processing schematics. Separate guides retain primary structural references and limits for each family. The detail inspector enlarges a captured existing SVG state; it supplies no additional atomic or experimental evidence. The registry refactoring and new recipes reuse the kit shell and preserve the role-based contour hierarchy. No source image or coordinate file is embedded by these additions.

Version 0.13.0 generalizes original schematic SVG actors developed during an authorized CRISPRi teaching-lesson revision. It adds a reusable molecular atlas, scale-normalized contour weights and semantic emphasis. RCSB structural presentations and primary scientific sources informed the shapes and biological explanations; their images and atomic coordinate files are not embedded or redistributed. Role-specific repression-protein shapes are author-created mnemonics. These are instructional states and geometry, not new structural measurements, a kinetic model or a molecular dynamics calculation. The specific reference mapping and limits are in [molecular.md](molecular.md). The scientific references retain their own attribution and terms.

Version 0.12.0 generalizes interaction fixes, label/glyph separation, recurring branched maps and verification techniques developed during the authorized b18 lesson revision. Its new example uses an original two-cell toy and calculated SVG; it does not distribute the supplied b18 report, private research data or competition scores. The result is reusable software and teaching guidance, not new empirical evidence. Version 0.12.1 further separates retained routes from replay animation and adds explicit attention for each teaching operation; the invented model and its scientific limits are unchanged.

Visual Lesson Kit is developed from locally developed course lesson implementations for CBIO7215, CBIO7102 and CBIO7105. Its runtime includes inherited and adapted presentation, localization and interaction code; the reusable examples and API helpers were developed across those local projects and this kit. Version 0.7.0 gives the active distribution a neutral name and its own documented visual system. These local implementations form the development history of the MIT-licensed library.

The MIT grant covers the original library software and documentation. It does not relicense cited publications, third-party fonts or structural archive data. Course-specific papers, recordings and private research materials are not included in this distribution; new lessons must preserve the terms of any source material they add.

The starter contains explicitly illustrative values and a generic illustration, not empirical results or figures from a research article. A new lesson must provide its own scientific sources. The [example catalogue](reference-patterns.md) documents the available APIs and teaching operations entirely within this library; other local presentations are not authoring dependencies.

Historical source-location records, hashes, references, verification reports and earlier design documents were preserved unchanged in a separate local history archive during the 0.7.0 transition. The maintainer can use that archive to trace earlier adaptations. It is excluded from the active distribution and is not required to create a lesson. The current code license is the root `LICENSE`; these notes preserve development history and third-party attribution.

## Fonts in the active distribution

Source Sans 3 and Source Serif 4 are selectable for lesson text and interface controls; Source Code Pro is used for code and monospaced text. All three Adobe families are distributed under the SIL Open Font License 1.1. DejaVu Sans is bundled as a fallback for otherwise missing mathematical glyphs under its supplied Bitstream/Arev notices, with DejaVu changes in the public domain. The exact supplied font files, source versions and visual roles are documented in [theme.md](theme.md). Complete copyright and license notices are included in `licenses/SourceSans3-OFL.md`, `licenses/SourceSerif4-OFL.md`, `licenses/SourceCodePro-OFL.md`, and `licenses/DejaVuSans-LICENSE.txt` at the lesson root, with the supplied-file record in `licenses/font-manifest.json`. These notices must accompany copied fonts, including embedded forms. The generator copies that directory into each lesson.

Preserve the supplied notices and font metadata. Font permissions do not license the lesson runtime, research figures or article text. Older font files and their notices remain with the historical versions that distributed them; they are not declarations about the fonts used by the active theme.
